db_02 - Boat rental (Easy)
==========================

Domain
------
A small Mediterranean boat charter company that rents sailboats, motorboats,
RIBs and catamarans by the day from a few marinas. The back office keeps its
fleet, its customers and the bookings in three MongoDB collections.

Collections
-----------
boats (5 documents)
  The rental fleet. Each boat has its commercial data (name, boat_type,
  manufacturer, model, year_built, length_m, max_passengers, cabins,
  engine_hp, daily_rate_eur, license_required, status) and its mooring
  location in the nested object home_berth. Sailboats and catamarans have
  cabins; the small motorboat and the RIB do not. "Sirocco" is in maintenance
  and has no berth assigned, so home_berth is missing in that document.

customers (5 documents)
  People who book boats: contact data, nationality, date_of_birth (missing for
  one customer) and registered_at. A customer who holds a boating licence has
  it stored in the nested object boating_license. Pieter van Roekel has no
  licence and only rents the licence-free motorboat "Pinnetta".

rentals (5 documents)
  One booking of one boat by one customer. It points to the boat (boat_id) and
  to the customer (customer_id) by ObjectId only, and stores the dates,
  passengers, optional skipper, totals, deposit and optional notes. A paid
  booking holds its payment in the nested object payment; the pending booking
  for May 2027 has no payment yet.

How the data and the patterns work together
-------------------------------------------
- Embedding (single nested object, the only form used in the database):
    boats.home_berth          - berth data belongs to the boat only
    customers.boating_license - licence data belongs to the customer only
    rentals.payment           - payment data belongs to the rental only
  None of these nested objects carries an identifier, and no collection stores
  berths, licences or payments as documents of their own.
- Referencing (single ObjectId):
    rentals.boat_id     -> boats._id
    rentals.customer_id -> customers._id
  The rental copies no attribute of the boat or of the customer. Totals are
  consistent with the boat's daily rate: total_amount_eur = daily_rate_eur x
  number of rental days (pickup day to return day inclusive), plus
  skipper_fee_eur when a skipper is included (rental on "Maestrale":
  7 x 690 + 7 x 180 = 6090). Passengers never exceed the boat's
  max_passengers, and licence-requiring boats are rented only by customers
  with a boating_license.

  Reference map:
    rental ...0301 -> boat ...0101 (Libeccio),    customer ...0201
    rental ...0302 -> boat ...0103 (Pinnetta),    customer ...0205
    rental ...0303 -> boat ...0104 (Maestrale),   customer ...0202
    rental ...0304 -> boat ...0102 (Gabbiano II), customer ...0203
    rental ...0305 -> boat ...0101 (Libeccio),    customer ...0204
  Boat ...0105 (Sirocco) is in maintenance and has no rental.

- No other pattern is present: there are no arrays at all in the database
  (so no Array, Subset or Bucket), and no object combines an identifier with
  copied fields (so no ExtendedReference).

Difficulty requirements applied (Easy)
--------------------------------------
- Every identifier field is named <entity>_id and holds ObjectIds:
  rentals.boat_id, rentals.customer_id.
- One form per pattern: references are single ObjectIds (never arrays);
  Embedding always uses a single nested object (home_berth, boating_license,
  payment), never an array of objects.
- At most one pattern other than Referencing per collection: boats has only
  Embedding (home_berth), customers only Embedding (boating_license), rentals
  only Embedding (payment) besides its two references.
- No pattern nested inside another: the three embedded objects contain only
  plain scalar fields and dates, one level deep.
- Optional fields missing in some documents: boats.cabins,
  boats.home_berth, customers.date_of_birth, customers.boating_license,
  rentals.skipper_included, rentals.skipper_fee_eur, rentals.notes,
  rentals.payment, payment.card_last4. Each pattern-carrying field is present
  in at least 4 of 5 documents.
