db_08 - Radiology examinations (difficulty: Medium)

DOMAIN
The operational database of an outpatient/hospital radiology department running two sites
(Riverside Imaging Center and Northgate Breast Center). It tracks patients, the imaging
exams they undergo, the scanners that acquire them, the radiologists who protocol and read
them, and the signed radiology reports.

COLLECTIONS
- patients (5 docs): demographics (mrn, name, birth date, sex, contact), a postal address,
  and a short list of the patient's latest exams for quick display on the patient banner.
- exams (10 docs): the full exam history. One document per accession: modality, CPT
  procedure code, body part, priority, status, acquisition time, contrast and dose. Links to
  the patient, the scanner and (for CT/MR/MG) the radiologist who protocoled it. Flat
  documents with no nested data.
- scanners (5 docs): imaging equipment (CT, 3T MR, DR X-ray room, breast ultrasound,
  tomosynthesis mammography unit) with its DICOM station AE name, vendor details and location.
- radiologists (5 docs): reading physicians with NPI, subspecialty and employment data. One
  (pediatric radiology, inactive) is not referenced by any exam or report.
- reports (5 docs): signed reports. One report may cover several linked accessions read
  together (CT chest + CT abdomen/pelvis, CT head + CT cervical spine, diagnostic mammogram +
  breast ultrasound). Each report lists its structured findings.

PATTERNS AND HOW THEY WORK TOGETHER
- Subset: patients.recent_exams[] holds copies of at most the 3 latest exams of the patient
  (by performed_at, newest first). The copies are identical to documents in the exams
  collection, which holds the full history and points back with exams.patient_id. Patient
  MRN-0048213 has 4 exams; her oldest one (XR chest, 2025-03-11, accession RIV25-031104) is
  only in the exams collection. Other patients have 2, 2, 1 and 1 exams, all copied.
- Referencing: exams.patient_id -> patients, exams.scanner_id -> scanners,
  exams.protocoled_by -> radiologists, reports.exam_ids[] -> exams (list),
  reports.signed_by -> radiologists. No attributes of the target are copied.
- Embedding: patients.address (single object), scanners.location (single object),
  reports.findings[] (array of finding objects: anatomic_site, observation, optional
  laterality and size_mm).
No Array, ExtendedReference or Bucket pattern is present: there are no lists of plain
scalar values, no copied parent fields next to identifiers, and no grouped time series.

DIFFICULTY REQUIREMENTS APPLIED (Medium)
- Role-named identifiers: reports.signed_by and exams.protocoled_by (both -> radiologists).
  All identifiers are ObjectIds.
- Mixed forms: single identifiers (exams.patient_id, exams.scanner_id, exams.protocoled_by,
  reports.signed_by) and a list of identifiers (reports.exam_ids[]); single embedded objects
  (patients.address, scanners.location) and an array of objects (reports.findings[]).
- A collection with 2 patterns other than Referencing: patients (Embedding on address,
  Subset on recent_exams[]).
- No pattern nested inside another: embedded objects and findings hold only plain fields.
  The exam copies inside recent_exams[] carry the exam's own identifier fields, but these
  belong to the Subset and are not labelled or listed as relationships.
- Different array lengths: recent_exams[] has 3/2/2/1/1 items, exam_ids[] has 2/1/2/2/1,
  findings[] has 3/2/2/2/3.
- Optional fields missing in some documents: patients.email, address.line2,
  exams.protocoled_by, exams.laterality, exams.radiation_dose_mgy (absent for MR/US),
  scanners.slice_count / field_strength_t / last_service_date, radiologists.phone,
  reports.amended_at, findings[].laterality / size_mm.
